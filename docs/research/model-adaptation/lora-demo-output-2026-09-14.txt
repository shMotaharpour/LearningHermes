Read the head-to-head first. Full fine-tune moved all 72
base weights; LoRA moved the equivalent of a rank-capped edit and beat it on
BOTH columns here — because this task's true edit is exactly low-rank and
invisible to the old domain, so the constraint cost nothing while full
fine-tuning's extra freedom went to fitting label noise. That is the bet you
make when you choose LoRA: that the edit is low-rank. This demo is the case
where the bet pays; the sweep below is how you check it on your own task.

Now the sweep, the capacity knob. Below the true rank the adapter cannot
express the edit it is asked for. At rank 2 the new-domain error bottoms
out. Above it, watch two columns move in opposite directions: train mse
keeps falling PAST the label-noise floor — that is noise being memorized —
while the new domain gets WORSE again and the old domain keeps drifting.
Rank is not 'more is better'. It is a capacity knob, and the right setting
is the size of the edit, which you find by sweeping, not by hoping.

The floor row is the third knob: base precision, a number that exists before
any adapter does. Compare the two qlora rows: more rank on the same
quantized base made BOTH columns worse — extra capacity just chased the
same noise harder. What shrinks the quantization error is more BITS, and
the error shrinks with precision before training even starts. If your
adapter has plateaued and the base itself is the thing that is wrong, no
adapter setting will fix it.
